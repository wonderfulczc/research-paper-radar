# Research Profile

## Primary Topic

Breakdown-discharge-based wireless sensing. The central technical chain is:

`stimulus or sensing module -> electric-field/discharge condition -> breakdown, spark, corona, or microplasma event -> electromagnetic pulse or damped oscillation -> wireless reception -> waveform, frequency, amplitude, attenuation, spectrum, or resonance feature -> sensing decision`

The radar should look for papers that can strengthen this chain through theory, device design, modeling, wireless readout, packaging, or system integration.

Important: identify this chain from the abstract as a whole, not only from exact title keywords. Strong evidence can appear as phrases such as `friction-induced electromagnetic waves`, `gas breakdown discharge`, `triboelectric effect`, `self-powered`, `wearable sensing`, `wireless communication`, `information encoding`, or `long-distance transmission`.

## Track A: Breakdown Discharge and EM-Signal Mechanisms

Include only when the paper can support wireless sensing. Useful concepts include:

- gas breakdown, air breakdown, microgap discharge, movable gap discharge, spark discharge, corona discharge, microplasma
- breakdown threshold, Paschen deviation, field enhancement, tip electrode, secondary electron emission, space charge, surface charge
- discharge current waveform, damped oscillation, displacement current, electromagnetic pulse, RF emission, spectral feature
- RLC modulation, LC resonance, frequency modulation, amplitude modulation, attenuation coefficient
- humidity, pressure, temperature, strain, distance, gas, flow, metal, human motion, or environmental modulation of discharge or EM signal
- MEMS discharge devices, flexible discharge structures, wearable integration, wireless receiving coils, antennas, oscilloscopes, RF receivers

Exclude ordinary discharge mechanism papers when they do not provide a plausible bridge to wireless sensing.

## Track B: Transferable Flexible Wireless Sensing

Include papers that do not use breakdown discharge only when they offer transferable ideas:

- flexible or stretchable wireless sensors
- passive LC, NFC, RFID, chipless RFID, SAW, backscatter sensing
- flexible antennas, resonators, electrodes, interconnects, encapsulation, wearable integration
- battery-free or self-powered wireless systems
- energy management, signal modulation, readout distance, anti-interference, multi-parameter decoupling
- self-powered or friction-induced electronic patches when the abstract states electromagnetic-wave generation and wireless sensing/communication
- self-powered electrotactile, textile, haptic, or human-machine-interface systems from top venues when the abstract states triboelectric effect plus gas breakdown discharge or another discharge/electromagnetic signal-generation step

The transfer value must be stated explicitly: readout mechanism, device architecture, packaging, modulation strategy, or system integration.

## Self-Powered and TENG Boundary

TENG, PENG, thermoelectric, photovoltaic, hydrovoltaic, moisture-electric, and hybrid energy harvesting papers are in scope only when they connect to wireless signal generation, wireless readout, battery-free operation, energy management, or discharge triggering.

Pure TENG output enhancement, triboelectric material optimization, or wearable TENG sensing is out of scope unless it helps the breakdown-discharge wireless sensing chain. However, top-venue self-powered systems should be kept when the abstract shows the mechanism chain: triboelectric/friction/electrostatic excitation -> electromagnetic waves or gas/breakdown discharge -> sensing, wireless communication, HMI, or wearable system function.

## Negative Scope

Usually exclude:

- pure triboelectric material studies
- pure TENG sensing without wireless discharge/readout insight
- pure material synthesis
- pure machine-learning prediction
- generic plasma physics with no sensor or EM-signal transfer path
- high-voltage insulation or power equipment breakdown with no transferable microgap, waveform, EM, or sensing value
- long review generation tasks
